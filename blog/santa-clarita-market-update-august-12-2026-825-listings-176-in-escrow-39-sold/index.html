The one thing to take away. Data drives real estate, and outdated data describes a market you are not standing in. On August 12, 2026 the Santa Clarita Valley carried 825 active listings, 176 in escrow, and 39 closings in seven days. Anyone quoting you numbers from spring is describing a different market.

Why does using current data matter so much in real estate?

Because data drives this market, and if you are looking at older data you are not going to understand the current market configuration. That is not a small distinction. Pricing decisions, offer strategy, and whether you feel calm or panicked all trace back to which numbers you are holding. A seller working off spring comparables and a seller working off this week's numbers are going to behave very differently, and only one of them is going to be right.

What is the difference between a condo and a townhome in Santa Clarita?

This is the question I get more than almost any other, and the answer is mostly about levels.

A condominium is typically a single level. Think of a rectangular box: usually no upstairs, no downstairs. You are generally going to have shared walls and shared ceilings with other units, depending on where you are in the building.

A townhome is typically categorized as having multiple stories. You might have a main level and an upper level, or you might enter near the garage, walk in from the garage into the dwelling, then head upstairs immediately to the living area. Diamond Head in Stevenson Ranch works that way. Some of the Vista del Canyon units over in Canyon Country do as well. Two stories means townhome.

Which floor should you buy on in a condominium?

There are a couple of schools of thought and they genuinely depend on who is living there.

  • Ground floor is usually better for anyone elderly or anyone who does not want to deal with stairs. It is also the easy answer for groceries.
  • Top floor means nobody above you. No neighbors, no pitter patter of little or big feet on your ceiling. The tradeoff is that you are hauling everything upstairs, every time.

Neither is wrong. Just know which tradeoff you are signing up for before you fall in love with a unit.

What should you check about parking and garage access?

Parking is the thing people forget to look at, and it varies enormously between complexes. Walk it before you commit.

  • Some units give you direct access into the home from your garage or from a parking structure. Townhomes are more likely to be configured this way.
  • Some of the Vista del Canyon units in Canyon Country have a private yard between the garage and your door. It is small, it is yours, and a lot of buyers love it once they see it.
  • Other Vista del Canyon configurations open the garage onto a common pathway, and you walk from there to your unit.

It is going to be very dependent on which complex you pick. Just understand there are different configurations and that this is a daily-life issue, not a detail.

Why do about 30% of Santa Clarita escrows fall out?

People hear that number and assume the market is in a tizzy, that something is wrong. It is really not the case. We have 176 properties in escrow right now, and roughly 30% of them will not close. That is just how the numbers are working out at the moment.

Most of the time it is nobody's fault. People assume it must be property condition, and it is really not that. It is spread across the board. The most common single reason is that buyers get cold feet. They decide they do not want to buy right now. Maybe they heard something on the news that convinced them. Maybe a relative intervened.

That is a normal feature of this market, not a symptom of a broken one.

How many homes sold in Santa Clarita in the last seven days?

39 real estate listings sold. Those are actual closed listings, not pending, not hopeful. You will see them reflected on the online channels and you will see them on SantaClaritaOpenHouses.com.

I also put 11 years of sold data up on the site so anybody can look at it. If you are a seller contemplating selling, you can go look at what actually happened rather than taking anyone's word for it, mine included.

Are short sales and foreclosures worth hunting in this market?

Short sales, foreclosures and bank owned properties are making up somewhere between 1 and 2% of this market. They are not common. And typically those properties go to investors.

So how do you compete with an investor? You would have to modify your loan, potentially go in cash if you have that capability, or shorten your time frames to make the offer more attractive. That is a real strategy, but understand what you are walking into.

There is also a quieter reality here. A lot of distressed property gets handled before it ever hits the market. Everybody is shaking hands under the table. It is a little like venture capital, where everybody wants to be the one injecting money into the next great thing, and the hard part is just getting on the list at all.

Why does everyone become a real estate expert when you start looking?

The moment you get interested in something, you see it everywhere. Buy a particular car and suddenly that car is on every street. Real estate is the same, except two things happen at once.

First, AI puts it into your feed. You start considering buying or selling and now Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, wherever you spend time, is all real estate all the time.

Second, the people in your life start talking. Your agent is the expert, sure, but now your whole family is the expert. The people at your job are the expert. They will tell you how they solved the world's problems as it relates to real estate and how they practically stole the house they bought, and how you can do the same thing.

Take what they tell you with whatever weight you want to give it. Understand that people often have a goal in telling you, which is to impress on you how sharp they were.

What should you actually do with advice from friends and family?

Do not dismiss it and do not obey it either. Bring it to your agent and ask.

Say it plainly: I have a buddy at work named Stan, and Stan told me he bought a property a couple years ago, a short sale, and picked it up for 60,000 dollars under fair market value. How does that work? Is there something there for me?

Then let your agent explain. Maybe that was the market then. I can absolutely hunt for short sales, foreclosures and bank owned property. But right now they are 1 to 2% of the market, and most go to investors. That is a real answer, and you only get it by asking instead of either ignoring Stan or blindly chasing him.

What happens to your information on the big home value portals?

If you are a seller trying to figure out what your property is worth, you can absolutely go to the online portals for information. Just be careful, and know the trade you are making.

Anything you give up on those systems is going to be sold to the highest bidder. And most of the people buying into those systems are real estate agents and lenders. They are trying to get your personal private information. That is the actual business model, and nobody puts it on the landing page.

That is exactly why my search site never asks you for your information to see data. The 11 years of sold data is just there.

How many Santa Clarita homes are in escrow? 176 properties were in escrow on August 12, 2026. Expect roughly 30% of those to fall out before closing, which is normal for this market.

How many homes sold in Santa Clarita this week? 39 listings closed in the seven days ending August 12, 2026. Those are recorded closings, not pending sales.

What is the difference between a condo and a townhome? A condominium is typically a single level with shared walls and ceilings. A townhome is typically multiple stories, often with garage access into the unit. Both are ownership types, and both appear in the 825 active listings.

Are there foreclosure deals in Santa Clarita right now? Very few. Short sales, foreclosures and bank owned property combined are 1 to 2% of this market, and most go to investors who can move faster or pay cash.
